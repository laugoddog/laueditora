How does our conscious thought work? How did it evolve? How does it relate to feelings, instincts, intuition and older structures in the brain-mind? How can we explain the control this type of high-order ability seems to enable? These are some of the questions Rafael Augusto tried to answer in this book, through a potentially unifying theory of cognition.

"Contrary to what might seem to the reader in the beginning, nothing in my words insinuates the nonexistence of deliberate reflection or the denial of the difference between one type of cognition and the other. This difference is not denied but reformulated. Above all, this difference is still “animal”—an extension, or complexification, of animality. While it is also more than that, or enables more than that—which also needs to be explained—what has been mostly ignored so far is not the rationality of this difference but the irrationality of this difference—which, nevertheless, has been present in our intuitions and narratives. Take, according to the Judaeo-Christian myths, the first memorable act by the first being who was born fully human: the murder of Abel by Cain. This was an act that, being more than animal, is founded in animality. Murder, taking a life, not for another instinctive need (like eating), but as an end in itself, is a uniquely human capacity, made possible not only by the consciousness of death but by the capacity for thought. As Massimo Recalcati says, “Violence does not erupt because man turns into a beast. In fact, the temptation to commit violence doesn't exist in the animal world” (2022: 58). To kill his brother, the world of instincts, emotions, and motivations had to become human. But this becoming human is not based on the existence of a cartesian Self or a higher system that controls, inhibits, and educates instincts; rather, it is based on a complexification of these instincts that can take various forms: Cain had to think about killing Abel. He had to boil in anger, envy, and repeatedly resent how his parents now looked at his younger brother. He had to feel jealousy, not only in Abel’s presence, but in his absence. He had to imagine the act before carrying it out. And all of this is uniquely human, all of this is thinking. Therefore, all of this must be taken into account when we aim to uncover what makes us thinking beings." — in Introduction.

PT

O que é a Prática Filosófica?

Muito antes da terapia, dos coaches ou dos guias espirituais, existia a filosofia — não apenas como exercício intelectual abstrato, mas como prática vivida. A busca de Sócrates, Séneca, Nietzsche ou Espinosa pela verdade não era meramente teórica, mas uma procura para transformar a ação e viver de forma intencional.

Hoje, a filosofia está a sair novamente das paredes da universidade para se reconectar com o concreto — com a vida. A Prática Filosófica — também conhecida como Aconselhamento Filosófico — está a ganhar nova atenção (como destacado no artigo do The New Yorker “When Philosophers Become Therapists”) como uma forma alternativa de busca espiritual que, em vez de se basear em crenças inquestionáveis, parte da dúvida, da abertura intelectual e da verdadeira curiosidade.

O Método

Cada filósofo tem o seu próprio método. O meu é flexível e começa pelas “pequenas verdades”: identificar padrões nas crenças e assunções — muitas vezes inconscientes — que moldam o teu pensamento. Partindo de uma questão específica, embarcamos numa jornada que te permite:

  • Compreender melhor a ti próprio, os outros e o mundo.
  • Organizar pensamentos confusos ou dispersos.
  • Descobrir as forças mais profundas que governam as tuas escolhas.
  • Explorar e resolver problemas com verdadeira profundidade.
  • Agir com plena consciência, propósito, responsabilidade e autonomia.

Isto não é terapia nem coaching. Não dou conselhos nem faço diagnósticos. Apenas te ajudo a ver as coisas com mais clareza — o que muitas vezes traz resultados extraordinários.

Quem sou eu para te guiar nesta jornada?

Doutorado em Filosofia pela Universidade de Edimburgo, Mestrado em Ciência Cognitiva pela Universidade de Lisboa, investigo a consciência e as suas ligações aos processos inconscientes, o pensamento reflexivo e as suas origens, tendo desenvolvido um modelo de cognição consciente que agora aplico a áreas como o livre-arbítrio, a responsabilidade, a agência e a metafísica.

Autor publicado de vários livros, além de um álbum musical e vários singles, sou apaixonado pela literatura e pela arte, como leitor e criador, tendo explorado múltiplos géneros artísticos e literários.

Formado em Prática Filosófica por Lydia Amir, uma das fundadoras do campo tal como o conhecemos hoje. Desenvolvi o meu próprio método, fundamentado na minha compreensão da mente e da natureza humana.

What is Philosophical Practice?

Long before therapy, coaches, or spiritual guides, there was philosophy — not merely as an abstract intellectual exercise, but as a lived practice. Socrates, Seneca, Nietzsche, or Spinoza’s search for truth wasn’t merely theoretical, but a quest to transform action and live intentionally. Today, philosophy is stepping out of university walls once again to reconnect with the concrete — with life itself. Philosophical Practice — also known as Philosophical Counselling — is regaining attention (e.g., as featured in the New Yorker article “When Philosophers Become Therapists”) as an alternative form of spiritual pursuit that, instead of relying on unquestionable beliefs, starts from doubt, intellectual openness, and genuine curiosity.

The Method

Every philosopher has their own method. Mine is flexible and begins with the “small truths”: identifying patterns in beliefs and assumptions — often unconscious — that shape your thinking. Starting from a specific issue, we embark on a journey that enables you to:

  • Better understand yourself, others, and the world.
  • Organize confused or scattered thoughts.
  • Uncover the deeper forces that govern your choices.
  • Explore and solve problems with genuine depth.
  • Act with full awareness, purpose, responsibility, and autonomy.

This is not therapy or coaching. I do not give advice or make diagnoses. I simply help you see things more clearly — which often has extraordinary results.

Who am I to guide you on this journey?

PhD in Philosophy by the University of Edinburgh, Master’s in Cognitive Science by the University of Lisbon, I research consciousness and its ties to unconscious processes, reflective thinking and its origins, and I have developed a model of conscious cognition that I now apply to areas such as free will, responsibility, agency, and metaphysics.

Published author of several books, plus a music album and various singles, I am passionate about literature and art both as a reader and a creator, having explored multiple artistic and literary genres.

Trained in Philosophical Practice by Lydia Amir, one of the founders of the field as we know it, I have developed my own method, grounded in my understanding of the human mind and nature.
